## Why Burn Rate and Runway Matter

A business can be growing and still face a cash shortage.

**Burn rate** tells you how quickly cash is being used. **Cash runway** gives you a simple estimate of how long your current cash may support the business if the present operating assumptions continue.

For founders without a finance team, these two metrics can provide a useful first view of liquidity and help answer practical questions such as:

- How much cash are we using each month?
- How many months of cash do we have left?
- How does revenue growth affect our runway?
- What happens if expenses continue to increase?
- When might we need to reduce costs, improve revenue or raise additional funding?

This calculator is intended to make those questions easier to understand without requiring you to build a detailed financial model.

---

## Gross Burn vs. Net Burn

**Gross Burn Rate** is the total amount of operating expenses incurred each month.

**Net Burn Rate** is the amount of cash the business uses after taking revenue into account.

For example, if a business spends **$120,000 per month** and generates **$60,000 of monthly revenue**:

- Gross Burn Rate = **$120,000 per month**
- Net Burn Rate = **$60,000 per month**

Net burn is particularly useful when thinking about runway because it reflects how quickly the available cash balance may decline.

---

## What Is Cash Runway?

Cash runway is a simple estimate of how many months a business can continue operating before its available cash is exhausted.

A basic calculation is:

```text
Cash Runway = Available Cash / Monthly Net Burn
```

The EasePro calculator goes one step further by showing a simple forward cash outlook based on the revenue and expense assumptions entered by the user.

This is still a **high-level planning view**, not a detailed cash flow forecast.
